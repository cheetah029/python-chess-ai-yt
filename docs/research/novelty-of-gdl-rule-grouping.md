# LGREF fills a gap that GDL decomposition left open

**Bottom line.** The research found no prior work that automatically groups the clauses of a single GDL description into overlapping gameplay-provision "rules" and then measures each rule's contribution by ablation with trained agents. That combination is novel and defensible for IEEE CoG 2027. The machinery is not novel. Dependency-graph clustering, hub removal followed by reattachment, weighting dependency types, and Louvain on code graphs all have clear precedents in software architecture recovery and in 1990s rule-base partitioning. The novelty therefore lies in four things: the **substrate** (clauses of a game logic program), the **GDL-specific edge ontology** (legal↔next links through a shared `does` action, temporal write→read across turns, negated-guard filtering), the **target unit** (a gameplay provision rather than an independent subgame or a software module), and the **downstream use** (rule-level ablation). The closest technical line, GGP game factoring, builds dependency graphs over ground fluents and actions and splits them into connected components to speed up search. On a single, non-compound game such as chess it returns one component and so finds no rule structure at all. LGREF's empirical case is weaker than its conceptual one. The margin over the name-token baseline is small on tic-tac-toe (0.711 vs 0.675 ARI), modest on the held-out game (0.378 vs 0.316), and larger only on nim (0.734 vs 0.525). The metric is a non-standard overlapping ARI, the resolution is calibrated per game, and there is a single labeller. Reviewers will press on all four points, so the paper should frame the grouping as an enabling, validated-for-feasibility component, not as a state-of-the-art clustering result.

## Five lines of prior work come close, and each differs on a decisive axis

The nearest neighbours fall into five lines of work. Each one shares one or two ingredients with LGREF and differs on the rest.

**GGP factoring and decomposition is the closest technical ancestor.** Günther, Schiffel and Thielscher, "Factoring General Games" (GIGA'09, pp. 27–33), build "a dependency graph between fluents and actions" whose connected parts are subgames, for single-player games only ([via Hufschmitt et al. 2016](https://jnv.up8.site/publication/hufschmitt-2016/hufschmitt-2016.pdf)). Cox, Schkufza, Madsen and Genesereth (GIGA'09, pp. 13–20) prove conditions for independent subgames using propositional automata (same source). Zhao, Schiffel and Thielscher, "Decomposition of Multi-player Games" (AI 2009, LNCS 5866, pp. 475–484), extend this to multi-player games. They must **ignore the control (turn) fluent, or every action gets linked together** ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/978-3-642-10439-8_48); [PDF](https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~mit/Papers/AI09b.pdf)). Hufschmitt, Vittaut and Méhat, "A General Approach of Game Description Decomposition for General Game Playing" (GIGA 2016; Springer CCIS 705, pp. 165–177), ground the rules, build a propnet-like circuit, and take connected components over "fully instantiated actions and fluents". They **decompose 32 of 40 games in under 5 seconds** and deliberately avoid syntax that a rewrite could remove ([full text](https://jnv.up8.site/publication/hufschmitt-2016/hufschmitt-2016.pdf)). A 2018 follow-up decomposes games statistically from simulations ([author page](https://vittaut.perso.lip6.fr/publication/hufschmitt-2018-a)). Cerexhe et al. (ECAI 2014) treat composition operators and leave detection out of scope ([PDF](https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~mit/Papers/ECAI14.pdf)). In every case the nodes are ground fluents and actions, not clauses. The partition is hard connected components, not modularity communities with overlap. The goal is search speed.

**GDL rule graphs and structure detection analyse whole descriptions, not their internal parts.** Kuhlmann and Stone, "Graph-Based Domain Mapping for Transfer Learning in General Games" (ECML 2007, LNCS 4701, pp. 188–200), turn an entire description into a coloured graph and match it against known games by isomorphism. They prove that isomorphic rule graphs mean scramble-equivalent descriptions ([PDF](https://cs.utexas.edu/~ai-lab/pubs/ECML07-rulegraphs.pdf)). Their "rule" is a single clause in the logic-programming sense. Kuhlmann, Dresner and Stone (AAAI-06, pp. 1457–1462) find counters, boards and pieces by "syntactically matching game description clauses to a set of template patterns", in order to build heuristics ([PDF](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~pstone/Papers/bib2html-links/AAAI06-ggp.pdf)). Clune (AAAI-07) and Fluxplayer (Schiffel and Thielscher, AAAI-07) belong to the same heuristic-construction family ([Clune](https://mlanthology.org/aaai/2007/clune2007aaai-heuristic); [Fluxplayer PDF](https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~mit/Papers/AAAI07a.pdf)). The method details given for those two come from memory in the notes and are **unverified**. Jung and Hoey extend rule graphs with edit distances ([PDF](https://ludii.games/citations/COG2021-1.pdf)). Their venue, CoG 2021, is **inferred from the file name and unverified**.

**Ludii ludemes are authored rule units; LGREF infers them.** In Ludii a game is a tree of predefined ludemes, which the authors contrast with GDL clauses that do not encapsulate designer concepts ([Piette et al., arXiv 1905.05013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.05013)). The roughly 500–600 hand-defined "concepts" label whole games ([Piette et al., CoG 2021, arXiv 2107.01078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01078v1)). Ludeme-based clustering groups *games*, not the rules inside one game ([Stephenson et al., arXiv 2105.12846](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.12846)). LGREF reverses the Ludii approach: it recovers ludeme-like units from a flat logic program where no such grouping exists.

**Rule-importance work in VGDL and rule-variant studies start from units that are already given.** Green et al., "Automatic Critical Mechanic Discovery Using Playtraces in Video Games", combine description parsing with playtraces to find the mechanics needed to win ([arXiv 1909.03094](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03094); venue unverified). But VGDL's interaction and termination sets already split the rules syntactically. Tomašev, Paquet, Hassabis and Kramnik, "Assessing Game Balance with AlphaZero" (arXiv 2009.04374; CACM 65(2), 2022), train AlphaZero on **nine hand-chosen atomic chess variants**. They measure decisiveness, opening entropy and piece values ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.04374); [CACM](https://m-cacm.acm.org/magazines/2022/2/258230-reimagining-chess-with-alphazero/fulltext)). This is the closest precedent for LGREF's ablation stage, and its units were picked by experts. The same holds for Ludi ([Browne & Maire, IEEE TCIAIG 2010](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/evolutionary-game-design)), RAPP chess-like generators ([Kowalski & Szykuła, EvoApps 2016](https://researchr.org/publication/KowalskiS16)), Variations Forever ([Smith & Mateas, CIG 2010](https://expressiveintelligence.github.io/papers/ieeecig20_vf.pdf)) and GAVEL ([Todd et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09388)). In all of them the rule units come from a grammar or from people.

**Software architecture recovery and rule-base partitioning supply the machinery.** Bunch clusters module dependency graphs under the MQ objective, which is modularity-like ([Mitchell & Mancoridis, IEEE TSE 32(3), 2006](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/tse/MitchellM06)). FICABOO clusters, detects omnipresent objects, removes them, reclusters, and reattaches them by orphan adoption ([Wen & Tzerpos, IWPC 2005](https://bibtex.github.io/IWPC-2005-WenT.html)). That is almost exactly LGREF's held-out board-update step, except that LGREF reattaches with soft overlap. Lutellier et al. show that the **choice of dependency type materially changes recovery accuracy** ([IEEE TSE 44(2); PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/lintan/publications/archrec-tse17.pdf)). Jacob and Froscher, "A software engineering methodology for rule-based systems" (IEEE TKDE, 1990), cluster production rules by the facts that flow between them and separate out control rules ([record](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/a-software-engineering-methodology-for-rule-based-systems/812458443427282946-2486); volume/issue unverified). Of everything found, it is the strongest single precedent for "group logic rules by shared facts".

## The pipeline is new; most of its parts are borrowed

The research supports four novelty claims, provided they are stated carefully. First, **no prior work automatically segments a GDL description into rule units of any kind**. Targeted searches for GDL dependency graphs with community detection (2023–2025) found only decomposition and verification papers ([He et al., KR 2024](https://proceedings.kr.org/2024/40/kr2024-0040-he-et-al.pdf)). No work applies modern community detection to a Prolog, ASP or Datalog clause graph. Second, the **typed edge ontology** has no analogue in call-graph clustering. Its GDL-semantic edge types are the legal↔next link through a shared action, the temporal next→true flow, terminal/goal co-dependence, and negated guards as a filtered class. The closest planning analogue is Fast Downward's causal graph, and that is used for heuristics, not clustering (citation unverified). Third, **overlap as a first-class output** goes beyond both FICABOO's single-cluster orphan adoption and the hard partitions of GGP factoring. Fourth, and most persuasive for a CoG audience, **pairing automatic rule identification with agent-based ablation** closes a loop that every rule-variation study leaves open, because each of them picks its units by hand.

Several things are not novel, and claiming them would invite rejection: graph clustering of a dependency graph (Bunch), removing hubs and reattaching them (FICABOO; the omnipresent-module idea credited to Müller et al. 1993 is **unverified**), weighting dependency types (Lutellier et al.), and Louvain itself. Louvain at default resolution is now a commodity step in code-intelligence tools ([infigraph-core docs](https://docs.rs/infigraph-core/1.5.5/infigraph_core/cluster/)). The turn-control hub that LGREF filters is the same hub Zhao et al. had to remove, so filtering ubiquitous fluents should be presented as a principled, weighted generalisation of a known fix, not as a discovery. The co-activation-from-traces edge, measured to hurt and then disabled, is worth reporting as a negative result. It contrasts usefully with the trace-driven methods (Hufschmitt et al. 2018; Green et al.), and it shows the edge set was ablated rather than assumed.

Three weaknesses cut against the claim. The **name-token baseline is close** on two of three games, so a reviewer can argue that GDL authors' predicate names already carry most of the signal. The answer is the held-out game and the robustness test described below. **Syntax dependence** is the established criticism from Hufschmitt et al.: a clause-level grouping can change when the description is rewritten. **Kuhlmann & Stone's scramble invariance** is the established benchmark for that concern, and the name-token baseline fails it by construction. Running that test is LGREF's cheapest decisive win.

## Position LGREF as rule identification for design analysis, not as a clustering advance

The recommended framing is that **LGREF lifts GGP decomposition from search to design analysis**. Factoring asks whether a game splits into independent subgames without loss, and the answer is binary and exact. LGREF asks which clauses jointly implement each gameplay provision, and the answer is approximate and overlapping. These questions are complementary, and the related-work section should say so in one sentence. The second framing sentence should place LGREF between Ludii and VGDL. Those systems hand designers rule units through their language, while GDL, a flat logic program, does not, and LGREF recovers such units so that rule-level analysis becomes possible on the GGP corpus. The third should note that Tomašev et al. showed agent-based rule evaluation is informative but required experts to choose the rules. The CoG contribution is then "automatic rule identification plus ablation", with the grouping validated as fit for purpose.

The related-work section needs five paragraphs. The first covers GGP factoring (Günther 2009, Cox 2009, Zhao 2009, Hufschmitt 2016/2018, Cerexhe 2014). The second covers GDL structure analysis (Kuhlmann 2006/2007, Clune 2007, Schiffel 2007, Jung & Hoey). The third covers Ludii ludemes and concepts (Piette 2020/2021, Stephenson 2023). The fourth covers rule evaluation with agents and mechanic discovery (Tomašev 2020, Jaffe 2012, Green 2019, Browne & Maire 2010, Hom & Marks 2007). The fifth covers software and rule-base clustering (Bunch, FICABOO, Lutellier 2018, Jacob & Froscher 1990), together with logic-program modularity. Splitting sets and module theorems define which decompositions are *semantically sound* but do not search for functionally meaningful ones ([Oikarinen & Janhunen, TPLP 2008](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.4582.pdf); [Janhunen et al., JAIR 2009](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/10622)). A cheap differentiator is to state that LGREF never splits a positive strongly connected component across non-overlapping rules, which ties the structural method to a semantic guarantee. Program slicing is the natural supervised alternative ([Szilágyi et al., ASE 2002](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/ase/SzilagyiGM02.html)), and seeding slices from each legal/next head would give an overlapping baseline.

## Reviewers will expect named overlap metrics, chance nulls and a native-overlap baseline

**The metric must be named.** Standard ARI is defined only for hard partitions, so "ARI by pair counting over soft membership" will draw the question of which generalisation it is. If two clauses count as agreeing when they share the same number of clusters, the measure is essentially the **Omega index** (Collins & Dent 1988, Multivariate Behavioral Research 23(2); confirmed only from a catalogue listing, so treat as partly unverified, [listing](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/1008654963)). If co-membership is a real-valued product or minimum of memberships, it belongs to the fuzzy Rand/Jaccard family of [Hüllermeier et al., IEEE TFS 20(3), 2012](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/tfs/HullermeierRHS12.html). The paper should give the formula and show that it reduces to ARI on hard partitions. The overlapping community-detection literature reports **NMI, Omega and F-score** ([Xie et al., ACM CSUR 45(4), 2013](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1110.5813)). Overlapping NMI should use the McDaid–Greene–Hurley "max" normalisation, because the LFK version can score unrelated covers at 0.5 or more ([arXiv 1110.2515](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1110.2515)). Pair-counting P/R/F fails several formal constraints that **extended BCubed** satisfies ([Amigó et al., Information Retrieval 12(4), 2009](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10791-009-9106-z.pdf)). Element-centric similarity handles overlap natively ([Gates et al., Sci. Rep. 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6561975/)). The cdlib library implements most of these measures.

**Chance correction matters most at LGREF's scale**, where there are few elements relative to the number of clusters ([Vinh et al., JMLR 2010](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/vinh10a.html)). With a fixed reference labelling, the one-sided random model of [Gates & Ahn, JMLR 2017](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v18/17-039.html) is the justified null. AMI is preferable to ARI when the reference has unbalanced, small clusters ([Romano et al., JMLR 2016](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v17/15-627.html)), and LGREF's references (one large board/legal rule plus small terminal rules) are of that kind. A permutation null with cluster sizes and overlap matched to the method's own output is easy to compute at tens of clauses.

**The baseline set should go beyond name tokens.** Reviewers will flag Louvain for producing **up to 16% disconnected communities**, which Leiden fixes ([Traag et al., Sci. Rep. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6435756)). They will also raise modularity's resolution limit ([Fortunato & Barthélemy, PNAS 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1765466)). The minimum credible set has five parts. The first is Leiden with the same overlap step, which isolates the overlap contribution. The second is a native-overlap method: link communities ([Ahn et al., Nature 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v466y2010i7307d10.1038_nature09182.html)) suit clause graphs, and OSLOM adds significance testing against a configuration null ([Lancichinetti et al., PLoS ONE 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3084717)). The third is trivial covers: all singletons, one cluster, and random covers with matched sizes. The fourth is an edge-type ablation in the spirit of Lutellier et al. The fifth is a **scrambled-names** run, which leaves LGREF's structural edges unchanged and should collapse the token baseline.

**Robustness and reference reliability carry the empirical case.** A resolution calibrated per game, and tuned on the evaluation games, is the most exposed design choice. The paper should report scores across a resolution sweep, together with the width of the region where LGREF beats the baseline. Any tuned setting should be chosen leave-one-game-out. Results should be given over 30 or more seeds. A second, independent labeller, with agreement reported in the same overlap metric, gives a human ceiling and answers the circularity objection. Software architecture recovery accepts reference sets of only **four to eight hand-verified systems** when the protocol is documented ([Garcia et al., ASE 2013](https://researchr.org/publication/GarciaIM13)), so three games can be defended as a feasibility result. Games that exist in both Ludii and GDL, tic-tac-toe at minimum, offer an author-independent second reference: map each GDL clause to the ludeme it implements ([arXiv 1905.05013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.05013)). No such alignment corpus appears to exist, so building one is itself a small contribution. On the purpose-built held-out game, targeted falsifiable predictions persuade more than an aggregate ARI of 0.378. An example prediction: the redundant clauses co-cluster under LGREF but not under the token baseline.

## Conclusion

The novelty claim against GGP is clean: decomposition finds subgames, and on a single, non-compound game it finds nothing. The novelty claim against clustering is thin, because the algorithmic steps all have names and citations. The paper should therefore lead with the *problem*, automatic rule identification as a precondition for rule-level ablation, and treat the clustering pipeline as an engineered, ablated solution to it. Two cheap experiments, a scrambled-names robustness test and a Ludii-aligned second reference, would turn the current baseline margins, which are uncomfortably small, into a structural argument that name tokens cannot make.

Unverified items to check before citing: the method details of Fluxplayer and Clune; the venue of Jung & Hoey; the venue of Green et al.; the volume and issue of Jacob & Froscher; the authors of the 1992 DKE knowledge-base-partitioning paper; Müller et al. 1993; Bunch's omnipresent-module feature; the Omega index details; the full text of the Günther et al. and Cox et al. 2009 papers, which were seen only through Hufschmitt et al.; the standard citations for Hubert & Arabie 1985 ARI, Blondel et al. 2008 Louvain, and Helmert 2006; and arXiv 2508.10438, "Repairing General Game Descriptions", which was not read. Coverage of the GIGA workshops from 2019 to 2024 was thin, so a dblp sweep of those tables of contents is the remaining novelty risk.
