# Prior work on evaluating individual game rules by ablation, rule variation, or variant comparison

Scope note: about 22 search/fetch calls. Most items were checked against an abstract or index page (arXiv, AAAI OJS, IEEE/Springer catalogue, author pages). Only Tomašev et al. was read in full text (first ~100k characters of the ar5iv HTML). Anything checked only from snippets is marked.

## Q1. Does any prior work identify a game's rules AUTOMATICALLY from a formal description before ablating or varying them, or are rule units always chosen by hand?

### Takeaway
I found no work that automatically splits a formal game description into rule units and then measures each unit's contribution by ablation with trained agents. In rule-variant evaluation studies, the varied units are either picked by hand (Tomašev et al.; Jaffe et al.) or fixed by a grammar the authors wrote (Ludi, Ludii ludemes, Variations Forever's ASP space, chess-like-game generators). Automatic structural analysis of formal descriptions does exist, but for other purposes. GGP game factoring splits GDL into independent subgames for search. VGDL "critical mechanic" discovery finds mechanics needed to win, to support tutorials and agents. Neither is followed by ablation-based contribution measurement or design advice.

### Cited Findings

**A. Variant comparison with strong trained agents (units chosen by hand)**
- Tomašev, N., Paquet, U., Hassabis, D., Kramnik, V. (2020). "Assessing Game Balance with AlphaZero: Exploring Alternative Rule Sets in Chess." arXiv:2009.04374 (cs.AI). AlphaZero is trained on nine chess variants, each an "atomic" rule change. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04374); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.04374)
  - Selection: the authors, with Kramnik advising, chose the variants by hand. They restricted themselves to atomic changes that keep the game close to classical chess, with no changes to the board, piece count, or setup. The paper says the variants "are by no means new to this paper" (several were existing named variants), and the aim was to find dynamic variants with new opening, middlegame, or endgame patterns. The paper does not describe any automated generation or identification of rules. Auto-balancing is mentioned only as future work. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.04374)
  - The nine variants: No-castling, No-castling(10), Pawn one square, Stalemate=win, Torpedo, Semi-torpedo, Pawn-back, Pawn-sideways, Self-capture. (v2 of the paper replaced one game, AZ-8, from v1.) — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.04374); [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04374v2)
  - Measures: decisiveness (draw rate and White's expected score at 1 s and 1 min per move); how often the new move types are used; opening diversity (entropy of AlphaZero's prior opening tree); KL divergence between opening trees; piece values from a linear model fitted to game outcomes; Kramnik's qualitative assessment of style. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.04374)
- CACM version: Tomašev, N., Paquet, U., Hassabis, D., Kramnik, V. "Reimagining Chess with AlphaZero." Communications of the ACM, Feb 2022 (vol. 65, no. 2, per the URL path). — [CACM](https://m-cacm.acm.org/magazines/2022/2/258230-reimagining-chess-with-alphazero/fulltext); [author PDF](https://ulrichpaquet.com/Papers/reimagining-chess.pdf)
- A search for 2023–2026 AlphaZero/MuZero studies that evaluate chess rule changes found no direct follow-up. One 2026 arXiv paper on asymmetric draw rules (repetition counts as a loss for White) cites the 2020 study, but I did not verify what it contains. — [arXiv 2604.03683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.03683)

**B. Balance via restricted agents (restrictions chosen by hand)**
- Jaffe, A., Miller, A., Andersen, E., Liu, Y.-E., Karlin, A., Popović, Z. (2012). "Evaluating Competitive Game Balance with Restricted Play." Proc. 8th AAAI Conf. on AI and Interactive Digital Entertainment (AIIDE-12), pp. 26–31. The method measures balance by playing restricted agents against unrestricted (optimal) ones. It includes a tool that computes balance measures for a family of games, applied to an educational card game. — [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12513); [researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/JaffeMALKP12)
  - This work restricts players' strategies (behavior), not game rules. As far as the abstract shows, the designer specifies the restrictions. I read only the abstract and metadata. Jaffe's 2013 PhD thesis at the University of Washington extends the work. — [UW thesis record](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/handle/1773/22797)

**C. Evolutionary/automated design over a grammar (units are ludemes or grammar terms)**
- Hom, V., Marks, J. (2007). "Automatic Design of Balanced Board Games." AIIDE 2007, pp. 25–30. A GA searches the space of game rules, run on the Zillions of Games engine. A game counts as balanced when self-play finds it equally hard to win from either side. The GA beats random search at equal compute. Rule units come from the Zillions rule language. (Author order differs between sources.) — [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/18777)
- Browne, C., Maire, F. (2010). "Evolutionary Game Design." IEEE Trans. Computational Intelligence and AI in Games 2(1), March 2010. DOI 10.1109/TCIAIG.2010.2041928. The Ludi system measures game quality automatically through self-play and uses it to evolve new games. Rule units are ludemes defined by the Ludi grammar. — [Maastricht CRIS](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/evolutionary-game-design); [IEEE catalogue record](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/1401797.htm)
- Browne, C. (2011). *Evolutionary Game Design*. SpringerBriefs in Computer Science, Springer. A book-length account of the same experiment. — [Springer](https://www.springer.com/gp/book/9781447121787) (verified from search snippet only)
- Kowalski, J., Szykuła, M. (2016). "Evolving Chess-like Games Using Relative Algorithm Performance Profiles." EvoApplications 2016, LNCS 9597. Game quality is the score gap between strong and weak AI players (RAPP), compared against human-made chess-like games. Rule units are the parameters of a grammar for simplified chess-like games. — [researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/KowalskiS16); DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-31204-0_37
- Todd, G., Padula, A., Stephenson, M., Piette, É., Soemers, D. J. N. J., Togelius, J. (2024). "GAVEL: Generating Games via Evolution and Language Models." NeurIPS 2024 (vol. 37, pp. 110723–110745). A code LLM trained on Ludii game code serves as the mutation operator in MAP-Elites. Fitness comes from automatic metrics, and novelty is judged with Ludii "concepts". Mutations act on Ludii code, so units are syntactic or ludemic, not identified rules. — [arXiv 2407.09388](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09388); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/c7b04e4e13bb77996d3ae2ff667231ac-Paper-Conference.pdf)

**D. Ludii / Digital Ludeme Project: evaluating reconstructed rulesets**
- The Digital Ludeme Project estimates ruleset quality from agent self-play: drawishness, game length, and first-player bias. The working assumption is that plausible historical rulesets avoid obvious flaws. Candidate rules are filled in at hand-specified "completion points" from ludeme fragments of related games. — [Report on the 2nd DLP Workshop, Browne, Piette, Crist, Stephenson, Soemers, ICGA Journal 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.3233/ICG-220211); [Report on the DLP, ICGA Journal](https://content.iospress.com/articles/icga-journal/icg190118)
- Crist, W., Piette, É., Soemers, D. J. N. J., Stephenson, M., Browne, C. "Computational Approaches for Recognising and Reconstructing Ancient Games: The Case of Ludus Latrunculorum" (Oxbow book chapter, 2024 per the author page). Combines historical evidence with AI self-play to judge candidate rulesets across board sizes. — [Piette publication page](https://piette.info/eric/publications/oxbow24-latrunculorum.php) (year from URL; venue details not fully verified)
- "Ludus Coriovalli: using artificial intelligence-driven simulations to identify rules for an ancient board game." *Antiquity* (Cambridge). Uses AI simulations to pick among candidate rulesets. — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/ludus-coriovalli-using-artificial-intelligencedriven-simulations-to-identify-rules-for-an-ancient-board-game/E5644BD43F8A5DC86DD1183A3E645ED9) (authors and year NOT verified)
- Piette, É., Stephenson, M., Soemers, D. J. N. J., Browne, C. (2021). "General Board Game Concepts." arXiv:2107.01078 (also IEEE CoG 2021 per Maastricht CRIS; venue not checked in full). Ludii "concepts" are high-level game properties, some computed at compile time from the ludeme description and some from playouts. The paper suggests concepts could help reconstruct rules and select agents, but says the link to agent performance is unproven. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.01078); [Maastricht CRIS](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/general-board-game-concepts/)
  - Relevance: concepts are labels derived automatically from a formal description, which is the nearest analogue to automatic rule identification. However, they are a fixed, hand-designed vocabulary of about 500 features (499 in the "Measuring Board Game Distance" database), not discovered groupings of clauses. — [Soemers et al. 2022, Measuring Board Game Distance (CG 2022)](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/files/171916581/Soemers-2022-Measuring-Board-Game-Distance.pdf)
- Stephenson, Soemers, Piette, Browne trained regression models that predict heuristic performance from ludeme descriptions ("General Game Heuristic Prediction Based on Ludeme Descriptions"). — [aitopics](https://aitopics.org/doc/arxivorg:40737C83) (year and venue not verified)

**E. Logic-programming design spaces (units defined by hand in ASP/event calculus)**
- Smith, A. M., Mateas, M. (2010). "Variations Forever: Flexibly Generating Rulesets from a Sculptable Design Space of Mini-Games." IEEE CIG 2010. An ASP generative space of mini-game rulesets, where designers "sculpt" the space with constraints. Rules are symbolic terms the authors wrote. — [PDF](https://expressiveintelligence.github.io/papers/ieeecig20_vf.pdf); [slides](https://es.slideshare.net/rndmcnlly/variations-forever-flexibly-generating-rulesets-from-a-sculptable-design-space-of-minigames)
- Smith, A. M., Mateas, M. (2011). "Answer Set Programming for Procedural Content Generation: A Design Space Approach." IEEE TCIAIG. — [course PDF](https://course.ccs.neu.edu/cs5150f14/readings/smith_asp4pcg.pdf) (snippet only)
- Smith, A. M., Nelson, M. J., Mateas, M. (2010). "Ludocore: A Logical Game Engine for Modeling Videogames." IEEE CIG 2010, pp. 91–98. An event-calculus engine that turns game rules into gameplay traces you can query. — [kmjn.org abstract](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Ludocore_CIG10-abstract.html); [PDF](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Ludocore_CIG10.pdf)
- Nelson, M. J., Mateas, M. (2008). "Recombinable Game Mechanics for Automated Design Support." AIIDE 2008, pp. 84–89, DOI 10.1609/aiide.v4i1.18677. A design is a logic specification that can be critiqued with logical queries. Mechanics are hand-authored, recombinable units. (The year is 2008, not 2007.) — [kmjn.org](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Mechanics_AIIDE08-abstract.html)
- Nelson et al. (AIIDE 2009) on prototyping rule systems in a formal language to get abstract playtraces. — [Playtesting_AIIDE09 PDF](https://www.kmjn.org/publications/Playtesting_AIIDE09.pdf) (title and authors not checked in detail)

**F. Parameter-space exploration (units are numeric parameters)**
- Isaksen, A., Gopstein, D., Nealen, A. (2015). "Exploring Game Space Using Survival Analysis." FDG 2015. — [bibtex](https://bibtex.github.io/FDG-2015-IsaksenGN.html)
- Isaksen, A., Gopstein, D., Togelius, J., Nealen, A. (2018). "Exploring Game Space of Minimal Action Games via Parameter Tuning and Survival Analysis." IEEE Trans. Games / TCIAIG 10(2):182–194. Parameterized Flappy Bird (gravity, pipe gap, speed and so on), automatic playtesting with a motor-skill player model, survival analysis, and data from more than 10^6 sessions. — [researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/IsaksenGTN18)
- "Discovering Unique Game Variants" (2015, often attributed to Isaksen, Gopstein, Togelius, Nealen; the authors are NOT verified in what I found). An evolutionary algorithm plus clustering finds Flappy Bird parameter variants that are far apart from each other. — [AMiner](https://aminer.org/pub/5f0e2a429fced0a24b94317a/discovering-unique-game-variants)

**G. Mechanic discovery and identification (the nearest automatic work)**
- Green, M. C., Khalifa, A., Barros, G. A. B., Machado, T., Togelius, J. "Automatic Critical Mechanic Discovery Using Playtraces in Video Games." arXiv:1909.03094 (2019, revised 2020; no venue on the arXiv page). Parses the game description (GVG-AI / VGDL) together with playtraces to find "critical mechanics", the ones that must be triggered to win. Checked against a user study of mechanics humans identified, and used to improve MCTS agents. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03094)
- AtDelfi (Green, Khalifa, Barros, Nealen, Togelius; arXiv:1807.04375, 2018). Builds a "mechanic graph" or atomic interaction graph from the VGDL file to generate tutorials automatically. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.04375) (author list from memory and the search summary; venue not verified, likely IEEE CIG 2018)
- Cook, M., Colton, S., Raad, A., Gow, J. (2013). "Mechanic Miner: Reflection-Driven Game Mechanic Discovery and Level Design." EvoApplications 2013 (Springer LNCS, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-37192-9_29). Uses program reflection to mutate code variables into new two-state mechanics, judged by simulated play. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/978-3-642-37192-9_29)
- Jara Gonzalez, J., Cooper, S., Guzdial, M. (2023). "Mechanic Maker 2.0: Reinforcement Learning for Evaluating Generated Rules." AIIDE 2023, DOI 10.1609/aiide.v19i1.27522. An RL agent is part of the fitness function for generated rules and produces more varied rules than A*. The authors also report the agent exploiting the fitness function. — [arXiv 2309.09476](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.09476v3); [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/27522)
- Zook, A., Riedl, M. O. (2014). "Automatic Game Design via Mechanic Generation." AAAI 2014. — [Riedl PDF](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~riedl/pubs/zook-aaai14.pdf); [arXiv 1908.01420](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.01420) (snippet only)
- Osborn, J. C., Summerville, A., Mateas, M. (2017). "Automated Game Design Learning." IEEE CIG 2017. Proposes learning game designs, expressed as operational logics, from interaction with game programs. — [arXiv 1707.03333](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.03333); [bibtex](https://bibtex.github.io/CIG-2017-OsbornSM.html). I could not verify a system named "Gamelan".
- "From Gameplay Traces to Game Mechanics: Causal Induction with Large Language Models" (arXiv:2602.00190, 2026). LLMs reverse-engineer VGDL rules from traces. This is the opposite direction: traces to rules. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00190) (authors not verified)

**H. Automatic structural decomposition of GDL (no ablation)**
- Günther, M., Schiffel, S., Thielscher, M. (2009). "Factoring General Games." Proc. IJCAI-09 Workshop on General Game Playing (GIGA'09), pp. 27–33. Builds a dependency graph of fluents and actions from GDL and takes its connected components as independent subgames. — citation as given in [Hufschmitt et al. 2016](https://vittaut.perso.lip6.fr/publication/hufschmitt-2016/hufschmitt-2016.pdf) (original not fetched)
- Zhao, D., Schiffel, S., Thielscher, M. "Decomposition of Multi-player Games" (2009, LNCS; DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-10439-8_48). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/978-3-642-10439-8_48)
- Cerexhe, T., Rajaratnam, D., Saffidine, A., Thielscher, M. (ECAI 2014). A systematic treatment of (de)composition in GGP. — [UNSW PDF](https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~mit/Papers/ECAI14.pdf)
- Hufschmitt, A., Vittaut, J.-N., Méhat, J.-N. (2016). "A General Approach of Game Description Decomposition for General Game Playing." Decomposes 32 of 40 games in under 5 s. — [author page](https://vittaut.perso.lip6.fr/publication/hufschmitt-2016/) (third author from memory; check against the PDF)

**I. Recent LLM-based rule and balance evaluation (2025–2026)**
- "RuleSmith: Multi-Agent LLMs for Automated Game Balancing" (arXiv:2602.06232). LLM agents self-play under a parameterized rule set, and a Bayesian optimizer searches the rule space. Includes an ablation table. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06232v1) (authors not verified; the rule space is parameterized by hand)
- "Game Arena: Strategic LLM Evaluation in Competitive Environments" (arXiv:2609.31473). Werewolf self-play with 500 games per rule configuration. One ablation found that disabling the Doctor's self-save barely moved the Villager win rate (73.4% to 72.4%). This is a single-rule ablation on a hand-chosen rule. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31473) (search summary only; not fetched)
- "Boardwalk: Towards a Framework for Creating Board Games with LLMs" (arXiv:2508.16447). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16447v1) (contents not checked)

### Inferences
- In every rule-variation study found (A, B, C, D, E, F, I), the varied unit is chosen by hand, fixed by an authored grammar (ludemes, the Zillions language, ASP terms, chess-like generator parameters), or is a numeric parameter. None groups the clauses of an existing formal description into rules without supervision before varying them.
- Automatic analysis of formal descriptions exists in two separate lines. (G) VGDL mechanic graphs and critical-mechanic discovery find mechanics relevant to winning, for tutorials and agent guidance. (H) GDL factoring finds independent subgames to speed up search. Neither assigns hypothesized strategic functions to rules, and neither measures each rule's contribution by ablation with trained agents. These are the closest prior art for component (1) of the framework and should be cited as such. The difference is the purpose (rule-level design analysis rather than search speed or tutorials) and the grain (rules within one game rather than independent subgames).
- Tomašev et al. is the closest prior art for component (3): variant comparison with trained agents, measuring decisiveness, diversity, and piece values. But each variant is an expert-chosen whole-game alternative, not a systematic ablation of every identified rule, and the recommendations are qualitative.
- I found nothing on objective-conditioned design recommendations (component 4) beyond single-objective fitness (balance, RAPP quality, Browne's aesthetic measures) and RuleSmith's optimization of a balance objective.
- Ludii "concepts" are the strongest counter-example to "always by hand": they are computed automatically from the formal description. They are still a fixed, hand-designed feature vocabulary, not discovered groupings, and I found no work that ablates them.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full texts of Jaffe et al., Browne & Maire, Variations Forever, Ludocore, or the Ludii reconstruction papers. Claims rest on abstracts and indexed summaries.
- I could not verify the authors of "Discovering Unique Game Variants", "Ludus Coriovalli" (Antiquity), RuleSmith, Game Arena, or the 2026 causal-induction paper. I could not verify a system called "Gamelan" (Osborn).
- No sources found on Hearthstone or Dota 2 (OpenAI Five) patch or rule-ablation studies, or on a "Gamesmith" system, in this pass. These may exist and were not searched in depth.
- I did not find a 2021–2026 Ludii study that explicitly ablates individual ludemes to measure their contribution to play quality. Its absence is not confirmed, and a targeted search of the Ludii publications page (ludii.games/publications) is recommended.
- 2023–2026 AlphaZero or MuZero chess-variant follow-ups: none found, and the absence is not confirmed.
